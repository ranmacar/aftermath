Resilience after catastrophe: the seed pods

A solar storm knocks out the grid. Cities can't cope. What survives is whatever was prepared.

In the story, someone saw it coming. In the last months, tens of thousands of shipping containers were buried upright in Faraday shells. Each one is a seed pod, not a bunker. Woken, it digs a shaft, casts a caisson around a central column, and raises a tower.

The idea underneath: resilience is distributed. Many small, self-sufficient places survive shocks better than one big system. At the mid-season climax, the Consortium's main base, around which the city of Arbolis is founded, launches its satellites, and the map lights up with every pod that made it, showing you were never alone.

Real-world anchors: vertical shaft sinking machines (4.5–18 m) and caisson sinking exist today. A pod that does it all in one pass is the fiction.

This is canon from the Arbolis novels; Aftermath is set in the fourth book.
