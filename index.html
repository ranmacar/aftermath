A slow browser game on the real map. Wake a buried seed pod, grow a tower, and tend one hex of land until it feeds people again.

Aftermath is a game set after a solar storm has taken out the grid. You wake a buried seed pod, it grows a tower, and you turn the land around it into a living farm, then connect with other towers. It's played on the real map, one real hex (about 1.5 ha) at a time, so the question it keeps asking is concrete: what would this place look like if people lived here well?

The current demo

In the demo you can:

  • dig out of a basement
  • follow a signal to a buried pod and find it on the map
  • build: solar, dig, rise, and watch the tower go up
  • farm the ring of round beds around the tower
  • forage nearby hexes and get home before a storm hits

Runs in the browser on phones and desktops. It's a prototype: tell us what breaks.

What it's about

Six ideas under the mechanics. Each has a short page.

  • Stewardship

    People can be a keystone species: the kind whose presence makes an ecosystem richer, like beavers or elephants.

  • Sanctuary, not conquest

    Most strategy games are about taking. This one is about keeping something alive.

  • Tree-like habitats and agroforestry

    The tower works like a tree: a deep root (a caisson, a buried shell of geopolymer concrete), a trunk (the central core), and a canopy (solar on top), with life spreading out around it.

  • Resilience after catastrophe: the seed pods

    A solar storm knocks out the grid. Cities can't cope. What survives is whatever was prepared.

  • The long game

    A deliberately big number, to change the question. Not "how few people can the Earth bear?" but "how could many more people live here while the land gets richer?"

  • Open design

    The things in the game should be buildable outside it.

What this is not

  • Not a city builder. One tower per hex, by design.
  • Not a survival shooter.
  • Not a building guide. Planning rules in most of Europe wouldn't allow a tower like this today; the game is partly an argument that they should.

What it is: a slow game about tending one place until it comes back. Start with one hex.

Roadmap

No dates, because we'd miss them. In order:

  1. Now: the demo. Dig out, forage, wait out storms and help the pod build its tower. Playable, still rough.
  2. Next: your home hex. Find other survivors, move into the tower, plant the ring, get the radio working.
  3. Then: neighbours. Contact reveals other pods; you can seed a second tower next door that runs on the first one's power.
  4. Later: airships. Trade between groups, multiplayer.

Alongside it: publish the CAD as open designs once they're tested enough to be useful.

What we're trying to do

  1. Make a good game first. If it isn't fun to tend a place and watch it come back, nothing else here matters.
  2. Show a different way to live on land. One midrise tower per farm, at most about 5% of the land sealed, the rest crop rings and food forest. The game lets people feel that before they argue with it.
  3. Stay close to reality. Towers, caissons, the Agrokruh ring (a ring-shaped farm layout), water handling: each has a real-world counterpart or an open design behind it. Where the game cheats (a pod that digs and builds itself), we say so.
  4. Feed the open designs. What gets modelled for the game (Onshape CAD) should be useful to people who want to build the real thing, starting small with a planter ring.
