# Aftermath: vision

Aftermath is a game in the **Arbolis** IP. Arbolis is the story world and also its main city, founded around the Consortium base. The game is set in the fourth Arbolis book and shares canon with the novels.

## The project in one paragraph

Aftermath is a game set after a solar storm has taken out the grid. You wake a buried seed pod, it grows a tower, and you turn the land around it into a living farm. Then you connect with other towers. It's played on the real map, one real hex at a time, so the question it keeps asking is concrete: *what would this place look like if people lived here well?*

## What we're trying to do

1. **Make a good game first.** If it isn't fun to tend a place and watch it come back, nothing else here matters.
2. **Show a different way to live on land.** One midrise tower per farm, at most about 5% of the land sealed, the rest crop rings and food forest. The game lets people feel that before they argue with it.
3. **Stay close to reality.** Towers, caissons, the Agrokruh ring, water handling: each has a real-world counterpart or an open design behind it. Where the game cheats (a pod that digs and builds itself), we say so.
4. **Feed the open designs.** What gets modelled for the game (Onshape CAD) should be useful to people who want to build the real thing, starting small with a planter ring.

## Why we're doing it

- **Most stories about the future are about ending.** Collapse, retreat, or a domed city sealed off from nature. We want one about repair: people as a keystone species, making the land richer by living on it.
- **Density and wild land don't have to be enemies.** Sprawl paves farmland; monoculture strips it. A tower that owns its own watershed and leaves 95% growing is a third option worth exploring.
- **Arguments don't move people; experiences do.** You can read that a hex can feed its tower. It's different to farm it.
- **Rules should judge results, not forms.** Permission by performance: you may live on land as long as the land keeps performing (little sealed, the rest productive or wild, checked over time). See [themes/open-design.md](themes/open-design.md) and [themes/stewardship.md](themes/stewardship.md).

## Principles

- **Each tower owns its environment.** Inside its circle everything is simple and centred on the tower: water slopes toward it, waste is handled on site. Larger landscapes are shaped only by networks of towers.
- **Living land, not pads.** Hexes stay soil, yard and canopy.
- **Sanctuary, not conquest.** No wave defense, no combat loop. Territory is the graph of hexes your power reaches, not paint you claim.
- **Survivors are residents, not units.**
- **Honest about what's fiction.** Label concepts as concepts and prototypes as untested.
- **Open by default.** Code MIT, hardware designs CERN-OHL-S-2.0, story/art/docs CC BY-SA 4.0. See the README.

## Story, kept light

A cyclic solar flare scars Utah and drives a global geomagnetic storm. In its last months, the Consortium buries tens of thousands of shipping containers upright inside Faraday shells. Each is a seed pod: it excavates, casts a geopolymer caisson around a central column, and raises a silo-like tower, one family per floor. In the game the Consortium stays unnamed until late. At mid-season the main base launches its satellites, and the map lights up with every pod that made it.

The same world is told in the Arbolis novels; Aftermath is set in the fourth book and shares their canon. The city of Arbolis grows around the Consortium's main base, the one that launches the satellites. Links to the novels will follow when their pages are ready.

## Roadmap

No dates, because we'd miss them. In order:

1. **Now: the demo.** Dig out of the basement, hear the pod, find it on the map, build (solar, dig, rise), farm the ring. Mostly there.
2. **Layer 0, the home hex.** Survive outside, forage nearby, recover survivors, feed the pod's stage gates, move in, plant the ring, repair comms.
3. **Layer 1, the mesh.** Uplink reveals other pods. Move the pod brain to an adjacent hex; the child tower runs on the parent's power.
4. **Layer 2, airships.** Factions, trade, multiplayer as an economy.

Alongside it: publish the CAD as open designs once they're tested enough to be useful.

## What this is not

- Not a city builder. One tower per hex, by design.
- Not a survival shooter.
- Not a promise that you can build this house today. In most of Europe you can't yet. That's part of the point.
